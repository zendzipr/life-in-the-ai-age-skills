# How to Use Idea to Launch

Idea to Launch helps you decide whether a business idea is worth pursuing and, if it is, turns it into a practical launch plan.

You do not need to prepare a formal business plan before starting. A short description of the idea is enough.

## What you need

Use an AI assistant that lets you attach files or add project instructions.

Add these files to the conversation or project:

- `SKILL.md`
- `templates/working-business-brief.md`
- `templates/business-launch-dossier.md`

If the AI assistant can search the web, allow web access when you want current competitor, pricing, market, or regulatory research.

## Start the process

Send this message:

> Use the Idea to Launch skill to help me evaluate and develop this business idea: [describe your idea]. Walk me through the process one step at a time.

A useful description might be:

> I want to offer a monthly bookkeeping service for small online shops in the United Kingdom. I have not spoken with potential customers yet, and I want to know whether the idea is worth testing.

## Answer the questions

The assistant will ask no more than three important questions at a time. It may ask about:

- the problem you want to solve;
- the customer you want to serve;
- the country or area where the business will operate;
- evidence you already have;
- your budget, time, or other limits.

It is fine to answer **“I don’t know.”** Unknown answers become research or validation tasks instead of being filled with invented facts.

## Review the checkpoints

The process normally pauses three times:

1. **Research framing** — confirm what market and competitors should be researched.
2. **Viability decision** — decide whether to continue, test, revise, or stop.
3. **Financial assumptions** — confirm the numbers before projections are created.

Read each checkpoint before choosing a path. The assistant should not quietly make important business decisions for you.

## Understand the verdict

You will receive one of four provisional verdicts:

- **BUILD** — the evidence supports a limited launch.
- **TEST FIRST** — the idea may work, but important assumptions need testing.
- **REVISE** — the current idea needs a meaningful change.
- **SKIP** — the available evidence does not support further investment in the current idea.

A verdict is a decision aid, not a guarantee.

## Ask for the final dossier

After the evidence and important decisions are ready, ask:

> Create the final Business Launch Dossier using the latest Working Business Brief. Keep assumptions, unknowns, risks, and source links visible.

The dossier can include the business plan, market research, competitor analysis, financial scenarios, launch plan, risks, performance measures, and a 90-day action plan.

## Check the result before acting

Before spending money or launching, confirm that:

- current claims include source links and dates;
- assumptions are labelled as assumptions;
- financial figures show their formulas and inputs;
- customer, price, costs, geography, and verdict agree across the document;
- legal, tax, accounting, medical, safety, or regulatory questions are reviewed by an appropriate professional.

If a major assumption changes, ask the assistant to update the Working Business Brief and regenerate every affected section before relying on the plan.
